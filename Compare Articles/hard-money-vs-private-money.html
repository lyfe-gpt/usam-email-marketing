Hard Money vs Private Money

Same asset-based idea, different source and flexibility.

Apply now

These terms overlap and are often used interchangeably. Both are asset-based loans secured by property rather than personal income, and both move faster and flex more than a bank.

The key distinction is the source: hard money originates from lending companies with structured programs, while private money comes from individuals or funds with negotiable, deal-specific terms.
Side by side
Dimension Hard Money Private Money
Source Lending company / fund Individual lender or fund
Underwriting Defined programs and criteria Often case-by-case
Flexibility Consistent, repeatable terms More negotiable per deal
Security The property (asset-based) The property (asset-based)
Speed Fast, process-driven Fast, relationship-driven
Best use Predictable, repeat borrowing Unusual deals needing flexibility
Bottom line

If you want consistent terms you can plan around, hard money is ideal. If your deal is unusual and needs custom structure, private money's flexibility can be the difference.
Common questions

Are hard money and private money the same thing?

Not exactly. Both are asset-based loans secured by the property, but hard money comes from a lending company like USA Mortgage with structured programs, while private money comes from an individual or private fund negotiating deal by deal.

Which is lower-cost?

It depends on the deal, not the label. A direct hard money lender with a structured program, like USA Mortgage, can often price more consistently than a one-off private arrangement, but an unusual deal may only pencil with a private lender's custom terms.
