SBA 7(a) vs Conventional Loan

Low down and long amortization, or fewer strings and faster.

Start my SBA file →

This is a trade-off between leverage versus speed. SBA 7(a) loans are government-backed with low down payments and long amortization but require more paperwork. Conventional loans are faster and simpler but typically need larger down payments and shorter terms.
Factor SBA 7(a) Conventional CRE
Down payment Often around 10% Typically 20% to 30%
Use of funds Owner-occupied RE, acquisition, working capital General commercial financing
Amortization Up to 25 years Often 5 to 10 years, may balloon
Backing SBA-guaranteed portion Bank holds full risk
Process More documentation, longer Faster, more straightforward
Best for Cash-preserving owner-occupiers Strong files wanting speed
Bottom line

SBA 7(a) suits those prioritizing cash preservation. Conventional works better for those with available capital seeking speed.
Common questions

Is an SBA 7(a) loan cheaper than a conventional loan?

It depends on the file and the lender, not the program alone. We place deals across a network of 20+ SBA lenders so you see real, comparable pricing instead of guessing which structure costs less.

Can I use an SBA 7(a) loan for investment property?

No. SBA requires majority owner-occupancy, so 7(a) is not built for passive investment property. If that is your deal, we can point you to the structure that actually fits it.
