Bridge Loan vs DSCR Loan

Short-term capital to reposition, then long-term money to hold.

Apply now →

These represent sequential financing steps rather than either-or options. A bridge loan provides short-term, interest-only capital for acquisition and repositioning. Once stabilized and rented, a DSCR loan becomes the permanent takeout based on cash flow qualification.
Category Bridge Loan DSCR Loan
Loan purpose Reposition before permanent financing Hold a stabilized rental
Typical term Short-term, up to 24 to 36 months 30-year amortization
Qualifies on The asset and the business plan Property rent vs. payment
Payment type Usually interest-only Principal and interest
Rate Higher, short-term risk Lower, long-term and stabilized
Best for Value-add, lease-up, cash-out Stabilized, rented properties
Bottom line

Use a bridge loan to buy and stabilize a property that cannot qualify for permanent financing yet, then refinance into a DSCR loan once it is rented and cash-flowing.
FAQ

Common questions on bridge vs DSCR.

When should I use a bridge loan instead of a DSCR loan?

Use a bridge loan when the property is not yet rented or stabilized, so it cannot pass DSCR's rent-vs-payment test. USA Mortgage bridge goes up to $10M with terms of 24 to 36 months and rates from 9.00%, sized for acquisition and repositioning, not for a property already producing steady cash flow.

Can I roll a bridge loan into a DSCR loan?

Yes. Once the property is rented and cash-flowing, USA Mortgage refinances your bridge into a DSCR loan from 5.5% interest-only on a 30-year fixed, using the rent to qualify instead of your tax returns.
