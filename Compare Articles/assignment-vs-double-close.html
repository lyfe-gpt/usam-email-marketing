Bottom line

Check the assignment route first. It costs you no purchase capital and skips a second closing when it's open. It closes the moment your A-side contract bars assignment without the seller's written consent, or your end buyer is using FHA financing. When it's closed and you still want the deal, the double close survives it, and USA Mortgage's transactional funding funds the A-to-B leg so you can close the B-to-C.
Frequently asked

Can I assign my contract if my end buyer is getting an FHA loan?

No. FHA Handbook 4000.1 requires the property be purchased from the owner of record, and the transaction may not involve any sale or assignment of the sales contract, regardless of the time between resales. A double close doesn't solve a same-day exit either: 24 CFR 203.37a makes the property ineligible for FHA insurance when the resale contract is executed 90 days or less after the seller's own acquisition. Confirm your end buyer's loan type before you sign the A-side contract.

Why would a seller refuse to let me assign the contract?

Sometimes the contract itself doesn't allow it. HUD's own REO sales contract, form HUD-9548, states the contract is assignable only by written consent of the Seller. Read your A-side paper before you plan your exit. If assignment is barred or restricted, a double close is the route that doesn't depend on the seller's consent. Have a local attorney review the contract either way.
