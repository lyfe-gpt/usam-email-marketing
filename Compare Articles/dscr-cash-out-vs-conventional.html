DSCR Cash-Out vs Conventional Cash-Out

Two ways to pull equity out of a rental you own.

Apply now

Both facilitate equity extraction but differ fundamentally in qualification. DSCR focuses on property cash flow; conventional examines personal finances.
Dimension DSCR Cash-Out Conventional Cash-Out
Qualification basis Rental property's income Personal income and DTI
Income documentation None required Tax returns, W-2s, pay stubs
Ownership structure LLC friendly Typically personal name
Property limits No cap Often capped
Typical max LTV Around 75 to 80% Around 75 to 80%
Best for Investors scaling portfolios W-2 earners with limited holdings
Bottom line

If the rental's rent covers the payment, DSCR cash-out gets you there without touching your personal income or DTI, and it closes in an LLC. If you have limited holdings and straightforward W-2 income, conventional cash-out is still a fit. The right structure depends on how the property performs and how you hold title.
FAQ

Common questions.

How much can I cash out with a DSCR refinance?

USA Mortgage DSCR cash-out is structured as a 30-year fixed with interest-only payments from 5.5%. Cash-out leverage typically runs a little lower than purchase, and the exact amount depends on the property's rent and current debt service.

Do I need tax returns for a DSCR cash-out?

No. DSCR underwriting is based on the property's rental income, not your personal income documentation, so tax returns, W-2s, and pay stubs are not required.
