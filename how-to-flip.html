How to flip a property: the plain-math version.

Flipping is not complicated, but the math is unforgiving. Buy right, budget honestly, and line up your money before you need it. Here are the four steps we see profitable flippers run every single time.

Apply now
1 Determine your budget and ARV

Start with the after-repair value, what the property will sell for once the work is done, based on real comps in that neighborhood. Then work backwards to your maximum offer. Most experienced flippers use the 70% Rule as the first filter:

The 70% Rule
Max Offer = ARV × 70% - Repairs - Closing Costs

If the seller wants more than that number, the margin usually is not there. Walk, or negotiate.
2 Analyze the deal

The 70% Rule gets you in the ballpark. The full analysis is every dollar in and every dollar out:

✓ Purchase price, plus acquisition closing costs
✓ Rehab costs, with a contingency for what the walls are hiding
✓ Loan fees and interest for every month you expect to hold
✓ Holding and selling costs: taxes, insurance, utilities, agent commissions

Subtract all of it from the ARV. A healthy flip targets a 10-20% return on your total investment. If the spreadsheet only works when everything goes perfectly, it does not work.
3 Secure, close, sell

Line up your funding before you are under contract, not after. A proof-of-funds letter (we provide them) makes your offer credible and lets you move on deals that will not wait for a bank. Then close fast, run the rehab on schedule, and get it listed. Every extra month of holding comes straight out of your profit.
4 Plan ahead

The best flippers do not find a contractor after they close, or a lender after they find the house. Build those relationships before the deal, not during it. When your crew and your capital are already in place, you can commit to a good deal in hours while everyone else is still making phone calls.
