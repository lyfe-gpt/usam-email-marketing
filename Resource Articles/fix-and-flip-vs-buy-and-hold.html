Fix and Flip vs Buy and Hold

One strategy pays you at the closing table in months. The other pays you every month for years. Neither is wrong, but the financing, the tax treatment, and the deal math are different. Here is how to decide which one fits your next deal.

Apply now →
The two strategies

Fast capital, or lasting cash flow.

Fix and Flip generates fast, active income: you buy, renovate, and sell, with profit realized in months at the closing table. It is an active model, and the income stops the moment you stop starting new projects.

Buy and Hold builds long-term wealth through rental cash flow, appreciation, and loan paydown over time. It is closer to passive once a property is stabilized, and the income keeps arriving whether or not you close another deal this quarter.
Side by side

How the deal math actually differs.

Fix and Flip
Payoff: Lump sum at sale
Timeline: Months, not years
Financing: Up to 90% purchase, 100% rehab
Taxed as: Ordinary income
Buy and Hold
Payoff: Monthly cash flow
Timeline: Years, compounding
Financing: 30-year fixed DSCR
Taxed as: Depreciation offsets rent
How to decide

Let the goal pick the strategy.

✓ Flip when you need capital now, whether that is to fund your next purchase, cover a gap, or simply take a profit off the table.
✓ Hold when you want income that compounds, and you can carry a property through lease-up without needing the cash back immediately.
✓ Do both with the BRRRR method: renovate like a flip, then refinance into long-term debt and hold, so one deal funds the next.
