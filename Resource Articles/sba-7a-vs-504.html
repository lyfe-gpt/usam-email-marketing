SBA 7(a) vs 504: which one fits your deal?

Both are government-backed loans for owner-occupied commercial real estate and business acquisition, and both can run up to 25 years. The right one depends on what you are buying and how you want it structured. Here is the plain-English breakdown.

Apply now
The common ground

Both are for property you occupy.

The 7(a) and the 504 are both SBA programs for owner-occupied commercial real estate and business acquisition. Both require majority owner-occupancy, so neither is for passive investment property. If your plan is to lease the whole building to tenants, an SBA loan is the wrong tool. If you are buying a building your business will operate from, read on.
SBA 7(a) General purpose

The flexible option: a single loan you can use across a range of business needs, from real estate to working capital to acquisition.

✓  One loan, one lender
✓  Flexible use of funds
✓  Best when needs are mixed
✓  Terms up to 25 years
SBA 504 Real estate & equipment

Built for fixed assets: it combines a bank loan with a CDC loan, needs roughly 10% down, and carries longer fixed terms.

✓  Bank loan + CDC loan
✓  Roughly 10% down
✓  Longer fixed terms
✓  Terms up to 25 years
