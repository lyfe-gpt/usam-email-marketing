Why investors choose interest-only.

An interest-only loan lets you pay only the interest for a set period, no principal. Lower payments, stronger cash flow, and more capital to put back into the deal. Here is how it works, where it fits, and the trade-off to plan for before you close.

Apply now →
How it works

Your payment covers interest, not the balance.

During the interest-only period, your payment covers only the interest that accrues each month. The principal balance stays exactly where it started. You clear it later, when you sell, refinance, or convert the loan to an amortizing structure. Until then, the payment is smaller than it would be on a standard loan, and the difference goes straight back into your deal.
Why investors use it

Three reasons it fits an investment strategy.

1 Stronger cash flow: on a rental, a lower payment means more net income each month, and that lifts the Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) lenders use to qualify the deal.
2 Lower carrying costs: on a short-term hold like a fix and flip or a bridge loan, you are not paying down a balance you plan to pay off in full at exit anyway.
3 Capital flexibility: the money you are not putting toward principal can go toward renovation, reserves, or your next deal instead.
The trade-off

You are not building equity through paydown.

Every dollar you save on the payment is a dollar you are not putting against the balance. That is the right trade for a short-term deal with a clear exit, appreciation, or a sale already in the plan. It is a worse fit for a property you intend to hold for decades, where paydown is part of the return. Know your exit before you pick the structure.
