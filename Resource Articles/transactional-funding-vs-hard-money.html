Transactional

Transactional funding or hard money?

They solve different problems, and the difference is not the size of the deal. It is how long you hold the property. Sell the same day and you need one product. Renovate and exit months later and you need the other. Here is how to tell them apart before you commit.

Start here

Your holding period decides the product.

Ask one question before anything else: how long will the property be in your name? If the answer is a single day, because a buyer is already lined up to purchase right behind you, that is transactional funding. If the answer is months, because you are buying to renovate before you sell or refinance, that is hard money. Get this backwards and you pay for capital you do not need, or you cannot close at all.
Transactional funding

One-day capital for a double close

It funds the A-to-B leg of a wholesaler's double close so the B-to-C sale can close immediately after. You hold title for minutes, not months.

✓Priced as a flat one-time fee, small relative to your assignment spread
✓No credit check, no appraisal
✓Built for wholesalers with a ready, funded end buyer
Hard money

Short-term capital to buy and renovate

A loan measured in months to acquire and rehab a property before you sell or refinance. Underwritten on the property and its after-repair value, not your personal income.

✓Rates in the high single digits to low teens, plus points (a point is 1% of the loan)
✓Funds the rehab through draws as work is completed
✓Built for Fix and Flip and other investor projects
How to choose

Match the money to the exit.

A same-day resale needs transactional funding. You have a signed contract to sell the moment you own it, so you only need the cash to bridge the two closings. There is no rehab to fund and no holding period to carry, so a short-term loan with points would just be dead weight. The safety of the deal hinges on one thing: a ready, funded end buyer. No B-to-C sale, no transactional funding.

A renovate-and-later-exit deal needs hard money. You are taking the property down, improving it over weeks or months, then selling or refinancing. That calls for a loan that carries you through the hold and releases funds for the work as you go. Trying to force transactional funding onto that timeline simply will not work, because it is one-day capital.
