What is a DSCR loan?

A DSCR loan is a rental property loan that qualifies on the income the property produces, not on your tax returns or W-2s. If the rent covers the payment, the deal works. Here is a plain-English guide to how it works and why investors use it.

Apply now →
What it is

One ratio does the qualifying.

DSCR stands for debt service coverage ratio. It is the property's gross monthly rent divided by its monthly debt service, meaning principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and any HOA dues. That single number tells the lender whether the property pays for itself.

A DSCR of 1.00 means the rent exactly covers the payment. Above 1.00, the property earns a cushion. Below it, the rent falls short of the payment, and the lender is counting on you or the deal structure to cover the gap.
How it works

The property qualifies, not your paperwork.

Because the underwrite is built on the property's rent, a DSCR loan skips the tax returns, W-2s, and employment verification a conventional mortgage demands. That keeps write-offs, self-employment income, and a complicated tax picture out of the way. At USA Mortgage, here is what that looks like in practice:

Why it matters to an investor

Built for how investors actually earn.

✓ Buy-and-hold landlords keep growing a portfolio without earlier purchases dragging down a debt-to-income ratio.
✓ Short-term rental investors qualify on the property's earning power when a bank will not count Airbnb income.
✓ Self-employed borrowers keep their write-offs between them and their CPA, because there is no tax return to hand over.
