How ground-up construction loans work.

A construction loan funds a build from raw land to finished home. The money is released in stages, you pay interest only on what you have used, and the loan is sized on two ratios. Here is how it works, start to keys.

What it funds

Land to finished home, in one loan.

A ground-up construction loan finances a build from raw land through to a completed home. Rather than handing over the full amount on day one, the lender releases the money in draws tied to inspected milestones, so the loan grows alongside the project. First-time builders are welcome.
How the money moves

Draws, not a lump sum.

Funds are released in draws as each stage of the build passes inspection, foundation, framing, and so on, rather than all upfront. Because you only draw what the project has reached, you pay interest only on what has actually been drawn, not on the full loan amount sitting idle.
How it is sized

Two ratios: LTC and LTV.

The loan is capped by two numbers, whichever is lower:

✓ LTC (loan to cost): the loan divided by the total project cost, land plus construction. USAM funds up to 85% LTC.
✓ LTV (loan to value): the loan divided by the finished value once the home is complete. USAM funds up to 70% LTV.
The exit

Sell it or refinance it.

A construction loan is short-term, a 12-month term on USAM builds, so it needs a planned exit. When the home is finished you either sell it and pay the loan off, or refinance into permanent financing and hold it. Either way, the construction loan does its job and steps aside.
