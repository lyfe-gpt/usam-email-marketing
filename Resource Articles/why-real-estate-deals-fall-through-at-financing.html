The deal is usually fine. The financing isn't.

Many investor deals that fall apart die at the financing stage, and the cause is usually a slow or wrong-fit lender, not a bad deal. Control that variable before your buyer goes under contract.

Talk to us

The common reasons deals die

Five reasons show up again and again, and none of them is the deal itself.

A slow lender: a bank or a slow private lender misses the contract date. The wrong loan type: a buyer tries to use consumer financing on an investment deal.

No proof of funds, so the offer never gets taken seriously. A buyer who was never pre-qualified, so surprises surface late and blow the timeline.

Title or insurance delays, the one variable nobody managed up front.

What to line up before the offer

Get your buyer with a direct, asset-based lender early, before there is a contract date to protect.

Secure a proof-of-funds letter before they make an offer, and pre-qualify the buyer so nothing surfaces late.

Then manage title from day one

Order title the moment you are under contract. It is the one variable most partners leave until someone else raises it.

When the lender underwrites in house and typically funds within 48 hours of clear title, title becomes the main variable left to manage.

Why this is your paycheck

If you wholesale or represent investor buyers, a dead deal is your lost paycheck.

Controlling financing is the highest-leverage thing you can do to protect your spread.
