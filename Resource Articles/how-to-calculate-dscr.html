Rental / DSCR

How to calculate DSCR, with a worked example.

DSCR is one division problem: monthly rent divided by the monthly payment. No tax returns, no debt-to-income math. Here is the formula, what counts as PITIA, and a real example so you can run the number on your own deal before you apply.

Apply now →
The formula

Rent divided by payment. That is it.

DSCR equals monthly rent divided by the monthly payment, or PITIA: principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and any HOA dues. A DSCR of 1.00 means the rent covers the payment exactly. Above 1.00, the property cash flows. Below 1.00, the rent falls short and you would need to cover the gap out of pocket. Most lenders want at least 1.00. USA Mortgage Fund goes down to 0.75, because a slightly tight property in a strong market can still be the right deal.
Worked example

Run the number on three scenarios.

$2,400 rent ÷ $2,000 payment 1.20 DSCR
$2,000 rent ÷ $2,000 payment 1.00 DSCR
$1,800 rent ÷ $2,000 payment 0.90 DSCR

The first scenario cash flows with room to spare. The second breaks even. The third is below 1.00, but still above our 0.75 floor, so it can still qualify. The fastest way to raise your DSCR on a specific property is to put more down: a bigger down payment lowers the monthly payment, which raises the ratio and can improve your rate.
