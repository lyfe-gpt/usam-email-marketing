What is ARV (after-repair value)?

ARV is what a property will be worth after the renovation is done. It is the single number a flip is built on: it sets your maximum offer and it caps what a lender will fund. Get it right and the deal pencils. Get it wrong and it never does.

Apply now
The definition

The value the property will hold once the work is done.

ARV is not today's as-is price and it is not a hopeful guess. You estimate it from recently sold, similar, nearby, renovated homes, the sold comps, not asking prices. Asking prices tell you what sellers wish they could get. Sold comps tell you what buyers actually paid for a finished house like the one you plan to deliver.
Why it drives the whole flip

ARV sets your offer and caps your loan.

ARV does two jobs at once. First, it sets your maximum offer through the 70% Rule, the fastest sanity check in the business. Second, it caps how much a lender will lend, often around 70% of ARV. Both come back to the same number, so an inflated ARV inflates your offer and your expectations at the same time.

That is why disciplined flippers underwrite the ARV before anything else. Everything downstream, the offer, the leverage, the projected profit, is only as good as that estimate.
The 70% Rule
Max Offer = ARV × 70% − Repairs

Take 70% of the after-repair value, then subtract your rehab budget. What is left is the most you should pay for the property. The 30% gap is your room for financing costs, closing, holding, and profit.

When the ARV supports the deal, USA Mortgage funds 90% of the purchase plus up to 100% of the rehab. A well-underwritten ARV is what earns that leverage, which is exactly why we build the calculator below around it.
