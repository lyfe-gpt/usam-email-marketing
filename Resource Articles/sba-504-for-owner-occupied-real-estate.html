Own your building with about 10% down.

The SBA 504 loan is built for owner-users. It pairs a conventional bank loan with a long-term, fixed-rate CDC loan so you can buy the building your business occupies, stop paying rent, and start building equity. Here is how the structure works.

Apply now →
How the loan is structured

Two loans, one purchase.

A 504 splits the project into two pieces. A bank or lender funds roughly 50% in first position. A Certified Development Company (CDC), backed by the SBA, funds about 40% at a long-term fixed rate. You bring the remaining down payment, often around 10%. That structure is what keeps the cash out of pocket low while locking in a fixed rate on the bulk of the debt.
✓  About 10% down ✓  Long-term fixed rate
✓  Real estate and equipment ✓  Purchase or construction
Who it fits

Built for owner-occupied property.

The 504 is for a business that will occupy the space, not for passive investment. To qualify, your business must occupy at least 51% of an existing building, or 60% of a new construction project. The property serves as collateral, but the operating business is the borrower, so underwriting looks at both the real estate and the business behind it. If you plan to rent out most of the building, this is not the right tool.
What to expect

A documented process, a fixed rate at the end.

The 504 is fully documented and usually runs 30 to 90 days from application to close, so it is not the tool for a deal that has to fund this week. What you get for that timeline is a low down payment and a long-term fixed rate on the CDC portion, which takes payment uncertainty off the table for years. If speed is the constraint, a bridge loan can carry you until the 504 is in place.
