The rate is only part of the bill

What decides a flip isn't the rate. It's total cost of capital over your actual hold, and the two biggest swing factors are points and how long you take.

Apply now

What you actually pay for

Five pieces make up the bill. Points, paid at closing, fixed, and unaffected by how fast you exit. Interest, which accrues while the loan is outstanding. Third-party closing costs: appraisal, title, escrow, recording.

Then the two people forget. Carry: taxes, insurance, utilities, and the loan payment, every month you own it. Exit costs: agent commission and seller-side closing, paid out of the sale.

Interest runs on drawn funds

On our Fix and Flip program we fund up to 90% of purchase and up to 100% of rehab. The rehab portion is drawn as work completes, so you aren't paying interest on the full rehab budget from day one.

Assume interest runs on the whole facility from closing and you'll overstate your cost badly, and the bigger the rehab, the worse the miss.

The month that costs the most

It's the one after your term ends. A 6-month term that runs to 8 months means extension fees on top of continued carry, and it lands exactly when your margin is thinnest.

Most flips that lose money don't lose it on the purchase price. They lose it on time.

Build the number before you offer

Take purchase, add rehab, add total finance cost over your realistic hold, add carry, add exit costs. Subtract that from a defensible ARV. What's left is the deal.

If the answer only works at your best-case timeline, it isn't a deal. It's a bet on the timeline.
