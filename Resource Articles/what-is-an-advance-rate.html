What an advance rate is

An advance rate is the percentage of a stated value that a lender will actually fund.

It is the same idea as LTV, said from the lender's side of the table. You will see it most often in commercial and portfolio lending.

The part that catches people

An advance rate is meaningless until you know what it advances against. Two lenders can both say 80% and fund very different amounts.

80% of purchase price is the number on the contract. 80% of appraised value may land above or below what you paid. 80% of the lower of the two is the most common actual rule, and the one least often quoted out loud.

Then there are the larger bases. 80% of cost means purchase plus rehab. 80% of ARV means the finished value, which is a much bigger number to take a percentage of.

So the useful question is never "what is your advance rate". It is "advance rate against what, and who determines that number".

Where advance rates show up

On a portfolio loan across several properties, the advance rate usually applies to the aggregate value of the pool rather than property by property. That is part of why a blanket structure can let a stronger asset carry a weaker one.

On a CRE bridge deal it applies to the as-is value, with a separate limit on total exposure once any capital works are funded.

Why the number moves

Asset type, condition, how liquid the market is, and your experience. A lender advances more against something they are confident they could sell.

That is the whole logic, and it explains most of the variation you will see between quotes on the same deal.
