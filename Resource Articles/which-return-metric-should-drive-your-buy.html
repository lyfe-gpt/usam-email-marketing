Which number should decide your buy

Cap rate, cash-on-cash and DSCR answer three different questions. Use the wrong one to decide and you can end up owning something that looks good on a spreadsheet and starves you of cash.

Apply now

Cap rate compares, it does not decide

Cap rate is NOI divided by price. It contains no financing at all, and that is exactly what makes it useful. You can compare two buildings without your loan terms distorting the picture.

It is also what makes it a poor decision metric. It tells you nothing about whether the deal works for you, at your leverage, with your cost of capital. Two investors buying the same building at the same cap rate can have completely different outcomes. Use it to compare. Do not use it to decide.

Cash-on-cash is what you earn

Cash-on-cash is annual pre-tax cash flow divided by cash invested. It answers what your money is earning, and it includes financing, which is why most investors should lead with it.

Its weakness is that it flatters leverage. Push leverage up, put less cash in, and cash-on-cash rises even as the deal gets more fragile. A high cash-on-cash on thin coverage is a deal with no margin for a vacancy.

DSCR is not a return metric

On a rental, DSCR is monthly rent divided by the full monthly payment: principal, interest, taxes, insurance and any HOA. At 1.00 the rent exactly covers the payment and nothing else. At 0.90 it does not. On commercial deals it is NOI over debt service.

It is a durability metric, not a return metric, and it is the number your lender qualifies on. Our DSCR program goes from 0.75, so a property can qualify while still needing support from you. That should be a deliberate choice, not a surprise.

Run all three, then add the costs

Cap rate to shortlist: is this priced sensibly for the submarket? DSCR to check survival: does it carry itself, and will it still at a realistic vacancy? Cash-on-cash to decide: is this the best use of the capital you are committing?

A deal that clears all three is worth pursuing. One that clears only cash-on-cash is leverage dressed up as a return.

None of the three include your total cost of capital on entry, or a prepayment penalty on exit. Add both before you commit. Not a commitment to lend.
