Two ratios, one loan

What LTV and LTC actually measure.

LTV, loan-to-value, is the loan divided by the property value. It answers how much you are borrowing against what the asset is worth.

LTC, loan-to-cost, is the loan divided by the total project cost, which is purchase plus rehab. It answers how much of your all-in spend the lender is funding.
The lower cap wins

Flips and construction are sized on both.

A lender running both a LTV cap and a LTC cap will lend the lower of the two resulting amounts. That is the number that protects the lender in either direction, whether the deal is thin on value or heavy on cost. So you need to run both before you make an offer, because the smaller one is your real ceiling.
Worked example

Take a $200K purchase with $50K of rehab, so total project cost is $250K, and an after-repair value of $340K. Your lender caps at 85% LTC and 70% LTV.

85% LTC of $250K total cost 0.85 × $250,000 $212,500
70% LTV of $340K value 0.70 × $340,000 $238,000
Lender funds the lower the LTC cap is smaller $212,500

You receive $212,500. On a $250K project that leaves $37,500 of cost for you to cover, which is your real down payment. Note that a point equals 1% of the loan, so any origination points are on top of that.
