Hard money loan rates and costs, explained.

Why it costs more

You are paying for speed, not a lower rate.

A bank underwrites you. A hard money lender underwrites the asset. That is why we can close in days where a bank takes weeks, and it is why the rate is higher. When a deal will not wait, the higher rate is the price of getting to the closing table in time. The trick is knowing every piece of that cost up front so nothing surprises you.
The three pieces

Where the money actually goes.

1 The interest rate: typically high single digits to low teens. This is the yearly cost of the money you borrow, and because hard money is usually interest-only, it drives your monthly payment.
2 Points or origination: a point is 1% of the loan amount, paid up front. Two points on a $300K loan is $6,000. Points are a one-time cost, not an annual one.
3 Closing costs: title, appraisal or valuation, legal, and recording. Standard on any loan, and worth budgeting for alongside the points.
The math

Your payment, and your true cost of capital.

Because the loan is interest-only, the monthly payment is simple: loan amount times the rate, divided by 12. A $300K loan at 9% is $300,000 × 0.09 ÷ 12, or about $2,250 a month.

Your true cost of capital is the points plus the total interest over how long you hold the loan. Weigh that number against the profit on a deal you would lose by waiting weeks on a bank. On most time-sensitive deals, the cost of the capital is small next to the cost of missing the deal.
