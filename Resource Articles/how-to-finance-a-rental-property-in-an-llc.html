Rental / DSCR

How to finance a rental in an LLC.

Holding rentals in an LLC keeps your liability and your title clean, and it is how most serious investors run their portfolios. The catch: not every loan will close in the entity. Here is which ones do, and how to qualify.

Apply now →
The short version

Conventional closes in your name. DSCR closes in your LLC.

A conventional loan is written to you personally, so the property lands on your own name and your own credit. Move it into an LLC afterward and you risk tripping the due-on-sale clause. DSCR and portfolio loans are built differently: they can close directly in the LLC, so the entity holds title from day one and your personal exposure stays limited. That is why most investors who hold more than a couple of doors run everything through an entity.
What to expect

Two things every LLC borrower should know.

✓ You qualify on the property, not your paycheck. A DSCR loan underwrites the rent against the payment. If the property carries itself, it qualifies, no matter how your personal tax return reads.
✓ A personal guarantee is still standard. Even with the loan in the LLC, expect to sign personally. The LLC holds title and shields the asset, the guarantee gives the lender recourse. That is the normal trade, not a red flag.
