What paying off a DSCR loan early costs

A DSCR loan is priced on the assumption you will hold it. Here is how step-down and yield maintenance work, and what each one costs if you sell before the term is up.

Apply now

Why the penalty exists at all

Long-term money is funded by investors who want the yield for a stated number of years. Paying the loan off early breaks the trade they bought, and the prepayment penalty is what compensates for that.

This is the single most common surprise on a rental refinance. It is also entirely avoidable, if you read the structure before you sign.

Step-down, the common shape

Step-down is the most common shape on investor rental loans. The penalty is a percentage of the balance that falls each year you hold. A 5-4-3-2-1 structure charges 5% if you pay off in year one, 4% in year two, 3% in year three, and nothing after year five.

The math is easy to run in advance. Take your expected payoff year, multiply the rate for that year by the balance, and you have the bill. The usual step-down runs three to five years.

Some programs offer a penalty-free option in exchange for a higher rate. That can be worth it if you expect to sell early.

Yield maintenance moves with rates

Yield maintenance is less common on single rentals and normal on CRE permanent debt. Instead of a fixed schedule, you pay the present value of the interest the lender would have earned had you held to term. It is not a number you can look up.

Direction matters. If rates have fallen since you closed, it gets costlier, because the lender cannot replace your yield. If they have risen, it can be close to nothing. A step-down is predictable; this is not.

How to plan around it

Know your real hold. If you plan to sell in 18 months, a 5-year step-down is the wrong loan, whatever the rate.

Ask what is exempt. Many structures allow a partial paydown each year, or waive the penalty on a genuine sale rather than a refinance.

Price it against the alternative. A bridge loan with no penalty may cost more in rate and less in total if the hold is short. If you are consolidating several rentals rather than refinancing one, the penalty applies per loan retired, which is often the argument for a portfolio loan.
