SBA loans: what the down payment and terms really look like.

For owner-occupied commercial real estate, an SBA loan usually needs around 10% down and amortizes over as long as 25 years. That combination puts a building within reach for operators who would struggle with a conventional 25% to 30% down payment. Here is how the numbers work.

Apply now
The down payment

Around 10% down on most owner-user deals.

Most SBA real estate loans call for a down payment near 10%, meaning the loan can cover up to 90% of the project on a suitable deal. That figure rises to roughly 15% to 20% for startups or special-purpose properties, such as a hotel or a car wash, where the collateral is harder to repurpose. Keeping less cash tied up in the building leaves more working capital in the business, which is the whole point of the program.
Terms and rates

Long amortizations that keep the payment manageable.

Real estate under an SBA loan amortizes for up to 25 years, well beyond the 5 to 10 year balloon common on conventional commercial debt. That longer runway spreads the payment out and protects monthly cash flow. On rates, the two programs price differently: a 7(a) loan is usually variable and tied to the Prime rate, while a 504 loan carries a long-term fixed rate on the CDC portion.

7(a) vs 504

Two programs, two structures.

The 504 program combines a bank loan with a CDC loan backed by the SBA, which is what delivers that long-term fixed rate on real estate and heavy equipment. The 7(a) program is the more flexible, general-purpose option, useful when you need working capital or a mix of uses alongside the property.
