Blanket loan or separate mortgages?

One loan across many doors, or a separate mortgage on each property. Both finance a rental portfolio; they just scale differently. Here is how to tell which one fits where you are today, and when it pays to switch.

Apply now
The short version

Same portfolio, two ways to finance it.

Individual mortgages are separate loans, one per property, each with its own terms and its own payment. A blanket loan is a single loan that covers several properties at once, underwritten on the combined cash flow, with one payment and a release clause that lets you sell any one door without touching the rest.
Individual mortgages

A loan per door.

✓ Often the lowest rate per property when each file fits the conventional box.
✓ Sell or refinance one property without touching the others.
− Conventional lenders cap how many you can finance, and the paperwork grows with every door.
Blanket loan

One loan, many doors.

✓ One payment across the whole group, and no cap on how many properties it holds.
✓ A release clause lets you sell one property and keep the loan intact.
− Usually a slightly higher rate than a clean conventional file on a single door.
When to switch

Small and rate-shopping, or scaling for real?

✓ Stay on individual mortgages while you are small. With a handful of doors, per-property loans usually win on rate and keep every option open.
✓ Consolidate around five or more properties. That is where conventional caps and the cost of managing a stack of separate loans start to outweigh the rate savings.
✓ Use a blanket to pull cash out. One loan across combined equity frees up capital for the next acquisition without refinancing each door on its own.

Bottom line: use individual mortgages while you are small and rate-shopping, and move to a blanket loan when scale and simplicity matter more than squeezing the last basis point on each door.
